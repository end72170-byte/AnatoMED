The mandibular alveolar bone appears as a well-defined semicircular hyperechoic band due to its dense cortical structure and high acoustic impedance relative to surrounding mucosa, muscle, and amniotic fluid. The echogenic arc is regular and continuous, without focal depression or interruption. Small internal hypoechoic spots represent early mandibular tooth buds.
After both the lower and upper alveolar arches are clearly identified, keep the probe at the same general horizontal level and avoid relying on simple cranial–caudal sliding alone. Gently rock the probe upward by a small angle so that the ultrasound beam is redirected obliquely into the oral cavity through the oral fissure. Hard palate visualization is usually optimal when the fetal facial axis is aligned with the beam direction, allowing the hard palate plane to remain nearly perpendicular to the insonation beam. If the facial axis is markedly oblique to the beam and the hard palate is not well seen, subtle lateral tilting of the probe may be helpful, with one side slightly lowered and the other slightly raised, in order to better align the beam with the fetal facial axis. Fine angular adjustment should then be continued to keep the beam as perpendicular as possible to the hard palate surface, thereby reducing attenuation and minimizing pseudo-discontinuity artifacts. With adequate optimization, a smooth horizontal echogenic band can be demonstrated along the roof of the oral cavity, consistent with satisfactory visualization of the hard palate.
